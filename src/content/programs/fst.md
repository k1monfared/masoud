## In plain English

The Federal Skilled Trades program is for people who work with their hands, including electricians, plumbers, welders, heavy equipment operators, and many other trades. It recognizes that Canada needs tradespeople badly, so the language requirement is lower than the other Express Entry streams and there is no points grid to pass. The main catch is that you need either a Canadian employer willing to offer you a job for at least a year, or a certificate of qualification issued by a Canadian province or territory.

## Who this is for

Experienced tradespeople who have been working in an eligible trade for at least 2 years in the last 5 years, and who either have a Canadian employer ready to take them on full-time, or who already have a certificate of qualification from a Canadian regulatory body. This program is well suited to workers who may not have formal post-secondary education but have real, documented skills.

## What you need

Two years of full-time paid work experience in an eligible NOC TEER 2 trade over the last 5 years. A valid full-time job offer of at least 1 year from a Canadian employer, or a certificate of qualification from a Canadian province or territory. Language test results of at least CLB 5 in speaking and listening and CLB 4 in reading and writing in English or French. No Educational Credential Assessment is required, and there is no minimum points grid to pass.

## How the process works

1. Confirm your trade has an eligible NOC code listed by IRCC.
2. Get your language test done and confirm you meet CLB 5 (speaking and listening) and CLB 4 (reading and writing).
3. Secure either a qualifying Canadian job offer or a certificate of qualification from a provincial or territorial body.
4. Create an Express Entry profile and enter the pool.
5. IRCC runs draws and sends Invitations to Apply. Federal Skilled Trades draws are sometimes run separately from all-program draws, so watch for both.
6. Receive your ITA and submit a complete application within 60 days.
7. Wait for a decision. If approved, complete your landing as a permanent resident.

## Things people get wrong

- Assuming any trade qualifies. Only trades listed under specific NOC groups are eligible. Check the current IRCC list before building your application around this stream.
- Counting self-employed trade work. Experience must be as a paid employee, not as a self-employed contractor, for the standard calculation.
- Thinking a foreign trade licence is the same as a Canadian certificate of qualification. Only certificates issued by a Canadian provincial or territorial authority count toward the requirement.
- Overlooking Federal Skilled Trades draws. Because draws for this stream are sometimes held separately, applicants with lower CRS scores may still get invited. Keep your profile current and watch for trade-specific rounds.
