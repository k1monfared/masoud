## In plain English

The Start-Up Visa gives immigrant entrepreneurs a path to permanent residence in Canada, not a temporary work permit that expires. The catch is that you cannot just declare yourself an entrepreneur. You need one of Canada's designated organizations, a venture capital fund, an angel investor group, or a business incubator, to review your idea and formally back you. If you can get that letter of support, the path to PR is open. We help you understand which organizations match your sector, how to approach them, and how to structure your application once you have a commitment.

## Who this is for

This program is for immigrant entrepreneurs who want to build a company in Canada and stay here permanently. It is not a passive investor visa. You are expected to actively manage the business. If you have a scalable business idea and are prepared to go through a competitive vetting process with a designated organization, this program is worth exploring seriously.

## What you need

- A business concept that a designated organization is willing to support
- A formal letter of support from that designated organization
- Language test results meeting CLB 5 in speaking and listening, CLB 4 in reading and writing
- Proof of settlement funds sufficient for your family size
- A business structure where you hold at least 10% of voting rights and where you plus the designated organization together hold more than 50%

## How the process works

1. Research designated organizations in your sector and begin building relationships early. Some incubators have open application processes. Others are invitation-based.
2. Pitch your business and go through the organization's review process. This can take months.
3. Receive a letter of support and a commitment certificate from the designated organization.
4. Take a recognized language test (IELTS, CELPIP, or TEF) and get your scores.
5. Gather proof of settlement funds.
6. Incorporate your business in Canada or prepare the incorporation documents.
7. Submit your permanent residence application to IRCC.
8. If eligible, apply for a work permit to operate the business while the PR application is processed.
9. Receive a decision on permanent residence. If approved, fulfill remaining obligations and land.

## Things people get wrong

- Approaching designated organizations with only a vague idea. They receive many pitches. A clear business model, a realistic market analysis, and some evidence of traction or expertise make a real difference.
- Underestimating how long the designated organization vetting takes. Many applicants start their IRCC application clock too late because they did not account for 6 to 12 months of relationship-building before the letter of support arrives.
- Language test scores that barely meet minimums. Higher scores strengthen the overall application and some processing streams reward stronger language ability.
- Not planning for settlement funds. Officers check that you can actually support your family on arrival, not just that the business might eventually make money.
