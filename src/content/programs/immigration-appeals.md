## In plain English

An immigration appeal is your formal chance to challenge a decision that did not go your way, whether that is a refusal to sponsor a family member, a removal order you received as a permanent resident, or a finding that you did not spend enough time in Canada. The Immigration Appeal Division is an independent tribunal. It is not the same office that refused your application. A member hears your case fresh and can overturn the original decision. The window to file is short after you receive the decision, so moving quickly matters. We can review what happened, assess the strength of an appeal, and represent you through the hearing.

## Who this is for

This process is for permanent residents, Canadian citizens, and protected persons who have received an adverse immigration decision that the IAD has jurisdiction to review. The most common cases are: a Canadian citizen or PR whose sponsored relative was refused, a permanent resident who received a removal order, and a permanent resident who was found not to have met their 2-year-in-5-years residency requirement. Not every immigration refusal is appealable to the IAD. Temporary resident refusals, for example, go through a different process.

## What you need

- The written notice of the decision you are appealing (refusal letter or removal order)
- The original application file and all correspondence with IRCC or CBSA
- Evidence supporting your appeal: documents, photographs, letters, financial records, country condition reports
- Witness statements if relevant
- For humanitarian and compassionate arguments: evidence of establishment in Canada, family ties, children's best interests, and personal hardship

## How the process works

1. Receive the decision and check the filing deadline printed on the notice. It is typically 30 days and strictly enforced.
2. File a notice of appeal with the IAD before the deadline.
3. Receive confirmation that your appeal has been filed and await scheduling.
4. Prepare your case: gather documents, prepare witnesses, and build your legal and humanitarian arguments.
5. Attend a case management conference if scheduled. This is a preliminary step to narrow issues and plan the hearing.
6. Attend the full hearing. Present your case, call witnesses, and respond to questions from the member and government counsel.
7. Receive a written decision. The IAD may allow the appeal outright, dismiss it, or stay a removal order on conditions.
8. If the appeal is dismissed, assess whether judicial review at Federal Court is an option within the 15-day window.

## Things people get wrong

- Missing the filing deadline. The IAD has very limited discretion to extend deadlines. If you miss it, the appeal right is gone.
- Not developing the humanitarian and compassionate case. Many people focus only on whether the law was applied correctly and overlook the separate humanitarian track, which can succeed even when the legal argument is weak.
- Coming to a hearing without organized evidence. An IAD member sees many cases. A well-organized record with clear labeling and a written summary makes your case far easier to follow.
- Underestimating the formality of the process. An IAD hearing is a legal proceeding. Preparation, presentation, and knowing the rules of evidence all affect the outcome.
