## In plain English

Category-based selection is a newer way Canada picks who to invite from the Express Entry pool. Instead of always picking the people with the highest overall scores, Canada can now run draws aimed at people in specific occupations or with specific language skills. So if you are a nurse, a French speaker, or a construction worker, you might get invited even if your overall score would not make the cut in a regular draw. Think of it as Canada saying "we really need people like you right now" and opening a side door.

## Who this is for

Anyone already in the Express Entry pool who works in a field Canada has designated as a priority, or who has strong French-language skills. The categories shift from year to year based on what the labour market needs. Past categories have included healthcare, trades, STEM fields, transport, agriculture, and French proficiency. If you are in the pool and your profile reflects a priority attribute, you are already being considered without doing anything extra.

## What you need

You need to be eligible for at least one of the three core Express Entry streams (Canadian Experience Class, Federal Skilled Worker, or Federal Skilled Trades) and have an active profile in the pool. Beyond that, your eligibility for a category draw depends on your occupation (using your NOC code) and your language results. IRCC also announces each year which categories are active, so the key is to keep your profile accurate and up to date.

## How the process works

1. Confirm you are eligible for CEC, FSW, or FST and create your Express Entry profile.
2. Make sure your NOC code is correct and your language scores are entered accurately, since these are the signals IRCC uses to identify you for category draws.
3. Watch for IRCC announcements about which categories are active for the current year.
4. Wait for a category draw. When IRCC runs one, it sends Invitations to Apply to candidates in that category above a certain CRS score.
5. If invited, submit your complete application within 60 days.
6. Processing follows the same steps as any Express Entry application.

## Things people get wrong

- Assuming you need to do something extra to opt in to category draws. You do not. If your profile is in the pool and matches a category, you are already eligible.
- Entering the wrong NOC code. If your occupation is in a priority category but your profile lists the wrong code, you will not be identified for that draw.
- Treating category draws as a guaranteed lower bar. Cut-off scores for category draws vary widely. Some are much lower than general draws, others are close to the same.
- Waiting to see what categories are announced before creating a profile. Create your profile now. You cannot be invited from a draw you are not in.
