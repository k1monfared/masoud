## In plain English

A visitor visa is what nationals of many countries need just to board a plane to Canada. It is a sticker in your passport that says Canada has reviewed your application and does not object to you visiting. Getting the visa does not mean you are automatically admitted when you arrive. A border officer still makes the final call at the port of entry. We help you put together an application that answers the officer's main questions before they are even asked.

## Who this is for

This program is for people who want to come to Canada temporarily for tourism, to visit family, or to attend a business meeting or conference. If your country is on the visa-required list, you need this visa before you travel. Even if you have a strong travel history, Canadian visa officers want to see a clear picture of your current ties and finances.

## What you need

- A valid passport (preferably with at least 6 months of validity beyond your planned stay)
- Proof of funds to cover travel and living costs during the visit
- A letter of invitation if you are visiting family (not always required, but helps)
- Proof of ties to your home country: employment, property, family, or other reasons to return
- Completed application forms and a recent photograph
- Biometrics (fingerprints and photo) if required for your country

## How the process works

1. Confirm that your country requires a visa and that you are not eligible for an eTA instead.
2. Gather your documents: passport, photos, proof of funds, and any support letters.
3. Create a MyCIC account and submit your application online, or apply at a Visa Application Centre in your country.
4. Give biometrics at a designated location if prompted.
5. Wait for the officer to review your file. They may ask for additional documents.
6. Receive a decision. If approved, a counterfoil is placed in your passport or you receive a letter of introduction for an e-visa.
7. Travel to Canada and be admitted by a CBSA officer at the port of entry.

## Things people get wrong

- Booking non-refundable travel before the visa is approved. Officers notice this and it can look like you are trying to force their hand.
- Thin proof of funds: bank statements that show a single large deposit the week before the application raise questions. Officers want to see funds that have been stable over time.
- Not addressing home-country ties. If your application is silent on why you would leave Canada at the end of your visit, the officer has no reason to approve it.
- Applying for a multiple-entry visa when you only plan one trip. There is nothing wrong with asking for multiple entry, but your application should make the reason clear.
