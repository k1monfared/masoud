  - q: How long does the LMIA last once approved?
    a: A positive LMIA is typically valid for 18 months. The foreign worker must apply for and receive their work permit within that window. If the permit is not issued in time, the LMIA may need to be reapplied for.
  - q: What is the Global Talent Stream and who qualifies?
    a: The Global Talent Stream is a fast-track LMIA path for tech companies hiring workers in specialized tech occupations. Approved employers can get a 2-week processing commitment. ## In plain English

If you run a business in Canada and you want to hire someone from outside the country, the LMIA is how the federal government makes sure you have genuinely tried to hire a Canadian first. You apply to Service Canada, show your recruitment records, describe the job and the wage, and wait for a decision. A positive LMIA is basically the government saying, "We agree there is no Canadian available for this role, and you can hire a foreign worker." Once you have it, your employee uses it to apply for a work permit. Without it, they generally cannot get a closed work permit.

## Who this is for

Any Canadian employer who wants to hire a temporary foreign worker under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program needs an LMIA for most positions. This includes small businesses, large corporations, farms, restaurants, care homes, and staffing agencies working with eligible employers. Some categories of workers are exempt from needing an LMIA, including intra-company transferees and workers covered by international trade agreements like CUSMA, but those are separate pathways.

## What you need

The key things you need are proof that your business is legitimate and operating in Canada, records showing your recruitment effort, the specific job offer with wage and working conditions, and the application fee. You also need to commit to a transition plan for high-wage positions, explaining how you will train or recruit Canadians for the role over time. Good documentation is critical because Service Canada assesses whether your recruitment was genuine.

## How the process works

1. Confirm that the position is not exempt from LMIA requirements and identify whether it falls under the high-wage or low-wage stream.
2. Advertise the position for the required period on the Job Bank and at least 2 additional recruitment channels.
3. Document your recruitment outcomes, including resumes received, interviews conducted, and reasons each Canadian applicant was not hired.
4. Submit the LMIA application to Service Canada with all supporting documents and the application fee.
5. Service Canada may contact you for additional information or a compliance review.
6. If approved, you receive a positive LMIA. Provide a copy to your foreign worker so they can apply for their work permit.

## Things people get wrong

- Treating the recruitment requirement as a formality. Service Canada expects real advertisements, real responses, and real reasons why Canadian candidates were not suitable.
- Advertising on platforms that do not attract qualified Canadian applicants. The channels used must be appropriate for the type of position.
- Using a stale or invalid LMIA. If the worker does not apply for a permit within the 18-month validity window, you may need to restart the process.
- Applying for an LMIA for a position that is actually exempt. Checking CUSMA occupations, intra-company transferee rules, and other exemptions before applying can save significant time and cost.
