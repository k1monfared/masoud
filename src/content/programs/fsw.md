## In plain English

The Federal Skilled Worker program is Canada's main door for skilled workers who have never worked in Canada before. Think of it as a points competition. You fill out a profile, Canada scores you on your language skills, education, work experience, age, and a few other things, and if your score is high enough, you get invited to apply for permanent residence. Most of your experience can be from anywhere in the world.

## Who this is for

Skilled workers outside Canada (or in Canada on a temporary status) who built their career abroad and want to start fresh as permanent residents. This program is especially useful if you have a strong language score and a university or college degree, because those factors push your score up significantly.

## What you need

You need at least 1 continuous year of full-time skilled work experience (or equivalent part-time) in the last 10 years, in a job classified as TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 under Canada's National Occupational Classification system. You also need a language test score of CLB 7 or higher in all 4 skills (reading, writing, listening, speaking). If your degree or diploma is from outside Canada, you need an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) from an approved organization. Finally, you need to score at least 67 out of 100 on the six-factor selection grid before entering the Express Entry pool.

## How the process works

1. Get your language test results (IELTS or CELPIP for English, TEF or TCF for French).
2. If your education is from outside Canada, order your Educational Credential Assessment from a designated organization and wait for the report.
3. Calculate your score on the 67-point selection grid to confirm eligibility.
4. Create an Express Entry profile and enter the pool.
5. IRCC runs Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) draws and sends Invitations to Apply to top-scoring candidates.
6. After receiving an ITA, gather all supporting documents and submit a complete application within 60 days.
7. Wait for a decision. Processing typically takes about 5 to 8 months from ITA for complete applications.
8. If approved, complete your landing and become a permanent resident.

## Things people get wrong

- Forgetting the ECA. A degree without an assessment means you claim zero education points, which often puts you under the 67-point eligibility threshold before you even enter the pool.
- Thinking self-employed hours count the same as employee hours. They often do not, and IRCC scrutinizes self-employment claims closely.
- Submitting employment letters that do not describe your actual duties. The letter needs to show that your role matched the NOC description, including duties, hours per week, and salary.
- Confusing the 67-point eligibility grid with the CRS score. The 67 points determine whether you qualify for the pool. The CRS score (which can be over 1,000) determines your ranking and whether you get invited. Both matter.
