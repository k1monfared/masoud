## In plain English

Saskatchewan is a growing province with real labour shortages in trades, agriculture, healthcare, and other sectors. SINP is the province's way of nominating people who fill those gaps. Compared to larger provinces, Saskatchewan's program is often more accessible for workers in mid-skill trades and certain semi-skilled roles. The fees are lower than most other PNPs, and some streams have lighter language requirements. If you have a connection to Saskatchewan through a job offer or a listed in-demand occupation, this program is worth a close look.

## Who this is for

SINP works well for workers in the trades, transportation, agriculture, and construction sectors who may not score highly enough for programs focused on high-skilled occupations. It is also a good fit for internationally trained nurses, engineers, and other professionals with Saskatchewan-specific job offers. Entrepreneurs who want to establish a business or buy a farm in rural Saskatchewan also have dedicated streams. If you already live in Saskatchewan on a work permit, the experience and ties you have built there count in your application.

## What you need

For the International Skilled Worker category, you need to either have a job offer from a Saskatchewan employer or fall into an in-demand occupation that does not require one. You need relevant work experience, language test scores at the stream minimum, and credentials that match the occupation. For the Entrepreneur stream, you need a minimum personal net worth, a detailed business plan, and a track record in business management. The Farm Owners and Operators stream has its own set of agricultural experience and financial requirements.

## How the process works

1. Review the SINP categories and check whether you qualify for the job-offer sub-category or the occupation in-demand sub-category.
2. Complete an online Expression of Interest profile with your personal and professional details.
3. Wait for an invitation from SINP through a draw or intake period.
4. Submit a full application with all supporting documents within the deadline.
5. SINP reviews your file and issues a nomination certificate if approved.
6. Apply to IRCC for permanent residence with your certificate.

## Things people get wrong

- Applying under an occupation that looks similar to yours but does not actually match your experience. Saskatchewan checks that your duties align with the NOC description.
- Submitting a job offer where the employer does not meet SINP's requirements. Not every Saskatchewan employer is eligible to support a nomination.
- Forgetting to have foreign credentials assessed before applying. Some streams require a completed assessment from a recognized Canadian body.
- Waiting too long to start the federal PR application after receiving a nomination. Certificates have an expiry date.
