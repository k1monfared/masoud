## In plain English

Ontario is Canada's most populous province, and it runs its own immigration stream to attract people the economy needs. When Ontario nominates you, it is telling the federal government that it wants you specifically. The program covers nine streams, ranging from tech workers with job offers to international students who graduated from an Ontario school. If you are already in Ontario on a work or study permit, this is often the clearest route to permanent residence.

## Who this is for

OINP is designed for international workers with skilled jobs in Ontario, graduates from Ontario colleges and universities who want to stay, entrepreneurs with a credible business plan for the province, and foreign nationals in the federal Express Entry pool who have strong ties to Ontario. If your occupation is in demand in Ontario and you have documented experience, there is likely a stream worth exploring.

## What you need

The requirements depend on your stream, but across most of them you need a full-time, permanent job offer in Ontario, work experience in an eligible occupation under the National Occupational Classification, language test scores at or above the stream minimum, and education credentials that meet the stream standard. Foreign credentials must usually be assessed by a designated body. Entrepreneur streams add a net worth requirement and a business plan review.

## How the process works

1. Identify the OINP stream that fits your profile using Ontario's stream selector tool.
2. Create an Expression of Interest profile with your relevant information.
3. Wait for a draw in your stream. Ontario invites the top-scoring candidates.
4. Submit a full application with supporting documents within the invitation window.
5. Ontario reviews your file and, if approved, issues a nomination certificate.
6. Apply to IRCC for permanent residence within six months of receiving your certificate.

## Things people get wrong

- Applying to a stream that does not match your actual situation. Each stream has specific criteria, and a mismatch leads to a refusal.
- Submitting outdated or unassessed foreign credentials. Ontario requires recognized credential assessments for most education-based streams.
- Letting the six-month window to apply for federal PR lapse after receiving a nomination.
- Underestimating the language requirement. Test scores must meet the minimum at the time of full application, not just at registration.
