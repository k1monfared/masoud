faqs:
  - q: How long will my PGWP be valid?
    a: Programs of 8 months to under 2 years lead to a PGWP valid for the same length as the program. Programs of 2 years or longer lead to a PGWP valid for up to 3 years. The exact duration depends on your program length and, for certain fields, your occupation.
  - q: Can I work while my PGWP application is being processed?
    a: If you apply before your study permit expires and had authorization to work during your studies, you may be covered by maintained status while you wait. You should confirm the conditions on your study permit and the IRCC guidance in effect at the time you apply.
  - q: Does the PGWP lead to permanent residence?
    a: Not directly, but it is a key stepping stone. The Canadian work experience you gain on a PGWP qualifies you for Express Entry programs like the Canadian Experience Class, which is one of the most common paths from study to permanent residence.
  - q: What if my school lost its DLI designation during my studies?
    a: If your school lost its eligible status after you started your program, you may still qualify if you enrolled in good faith before the change. This is a complex area, and the rules have evolved. We recommend a consultation to review your specific situation.

## In plain English

You worked hard to graduate from a Canadian school. The PGWP is your reward: a work permit that lets you stay in Canada and work for any employer, in any field, for a period tied to how long your program was. There is no job offer required, no employer sponsor. You pick where you work. Most graduates use this time to build Canadian experience, which is the single most valuable thing for getting permanent residence later. Think of the PGWP as the bridge between your student years and your permanent life in Canada.

## Who this is for

The PGWP is for international students who just finished a program of at least 8 months at an eligible Canadian institution. This includes college diploma students, university undergraduates, and graduate students. Short certificate programs, some English language schools, and institutions that lost their eligible status do not qualify. If you are about to finish your studies, you should check your school's eligibility before assuming you qualify.

## What you need

You need proof of graduation from an eligible program, including your final transcript or letter confirming completion, your passport, and your study permit. You should apply within 180 days of getting your final grades. If your study permit has already expired, you may need to apply from outside Canada, so timing matters. Your program must have been full-time during your enrollment.

## How the process works

1. Receive your final grades or official transcript confirming program completion.
2. Apply online through your IRCC secure account within 180 days of completion.
3. Include proof of study, your graduation document, and identity documents.
4. If your study permit is still valid and you had work authorization, you can often continue working while waiting.
5. IRCC issues an open work permit valid for the duration tied to your program length.

## Things people get wrong

- Missing the 180-day application window. There are no extensions to this deadline.
- Graduating from a program that is not on the eligible institutions list. Not every school in Canada qualifies, and this is a hard exclusion.
- Applying while the study permit has already expired without understanding the implications for status in Canada.
- Assuming the PGWP length will always be 3 years. Length depends on your specific program duration, and the rules for certain occupational programs have changed in recent years.
