## In plain English

Sponsoring your parents or grandparents for permanent residence means they could eventually live in Canada permanently, not just visit. Canada allows this but strictly limits how many sponsorships go through each year. Think of it like a lottery crossed with a qualification test: you have to meet an income requirement, then enter an annual intake process, and if selected, you can submit a full application. It is a long road, often several years, but for many families it is worth it.

## Who this is for

Canadian citizens and permanent residents who want their parents or grandparents to have the right to live in Canada permanently. You must be financially stable enough to meet the income thresholds across 3 years of tax filings, and you must be prepared to commit to supporting them financially for 20 years under a legal undertaking.

## What you need

You need to be at least 18 years old and a Canadian citizen or permanent resident. Your income for the 3 most recent tax years must meet or exceed the Minimum Necessary Income level set by IRCC for your household size, including the people you are sponsoring. You then need to be selected during an annual intake process before you can submit a full application. Once approved, you sign a 20-year undertaking agreeing to cover basic needs if the sponsored person cannot do so themselves.

## How the process works

1. Each year, IRCC announces an intake window for parents and grandparents sponsorship. Watch the IRCC website and be ready to submit an Interest to Sponsor form as soon as it opens.
2. If selected from the intake pool, IRCC sends you an invitation to apply. You then have a set deadline to submit the full sponsorship and permanent residence application package.
3. Submit the sponsor application (your income documents, undertaking forms) and the principal applicant's permanent residence application together.
4. IRCC processes the application, which includes health exams, security and criminality checks, and document review for your parents or grandparents.
5. If approved, your parents or grandparents receive Confirmation of Permanent Residence and can land in Canada.

## Things people get wrong

- Missing the intake window. It can open and close within days. If you miss it, you wait another year. Set a reminder and monitor IRCC announcements.
- Calculating income incorrectly. The MNI is based on your household size after adding the people you are sponsoring. Many sponsors forget to count correctly and discover mid-application that they fall short.
- Confusing the Super Visa with permanent residence. The Super Visa is faster and lets parents or grandparents stay for years at a time, but it is not permanent residence. It can be a good bridge while waiting for sponsorship.
- Underestimating the 20-year undertaking. This is a real legal obligation. If your parents or grandparents ever receive government social assistance during those 20 years, you may be required to repay it.
