## In plain English

The Canadian Experience Class is for people who have already worked in Canada and want to make that experience count toward permanent residence. If you spent the last few years working here in a skilled job and you can prove your language skills, this is likely your most straightforward path. You do not need a job offer and you do not need to have your foreign education assessed. Canada is basically saying: you already showed us you can work here, so let us make it official.

## Who this is for

People who have been working in Canada in the past 3 years in a skilled occupation, whether on a work permit, a post-graduation work permit, or any other authorized status. This is one of the most popular Express Entry streams because it rewards actual Canadian experience rather than a points calculation based on what you did abroad.

## What you need

You need at least 1 year of full-time skilled work in Canada, completed within the last 3 years, in an occupation classified as NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3. You also need to pass a language test in English or French. The minimum score depends on your occupation: CLB 7 for higher-skilled jobs (TEER 0 or 1) and CLB 5 for intermediate skilled jobs (TEER 2 or 3). You do not need proof of settlement funds and you do not need a job offer.

## How the process works

1. Take your language test (IELTS, CELPIP for English, or TEF/TCF for French) and get your results.
2. Gather your employment records: pay stubs, T4s, reference letters, and ROEs showing your job title, duties, and hours.
3. Create an Express Entry profile online and select CEC as your eligible stream.
4. Wait for a draw. IRCC runs draws periodically and invites the top-scoring candidates. Invited candidates receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA).
5. After receiving an ITA, you have 60 days to submit a complete application with all supporting documents.
6. IRCC reviews the application. If approved, you receive Confirmation of Permanent Residence and can land as a permanent resident.

## Things people get wrong

- Counting co-op or internship hours. Work done as part of a full-time academic program does not qualify, even if you were paid.
- Assuming a higher language score does not matter after meeting the minimum. Every extra CLB point above the minimum typically adds CRS points, which directly affects whether you get invited.
- Submitting reference letters that only list job titles with no duties described. IRCC needs to see that your work matched the NOC description. Generic letters get flagged.
- Letting the 60-day ITA window slip. Once you receive an invitation, the clock starts. Start organizing documents as soon as your profile is in the pool.
