## In plain English

BC runs its own immigration program because the province gets to nominate people it wants to attract. Think of it as BC raising its hand and saying, "We want this person." Getting that nomination does not give you permanent residence on its own, but it makes your federal application much stronger, or in the Express Entry lane, almost guaranteed. If you have a solid job offer in BC or a degree from a BC school, this is often the most direct path to staying permanently.

## Who this is for

This program is built for people who already have a connection to BC, whether that is a job offer, a work permit, or a graduation from a BC college or university. It also has streams for entrepreneurs who want to start or buy a business in the province. If you are a healthcare worker, a tech professional, or a tradesperson, BC has specific streams designed with your occupation in mind.

## What you need

The core requirement is a genuine connection to BC's labour market. For most streams that means a full-time, permanent job offer from a BC employer in an eligible occupation, a wage at or above the stream minimum, and relevant work experience. You also need to show language ability at the level set for your stream. The Tech stream covers a list of in-demand technology occupations and has somewhat different criteria. Post-graduation streams focus on your BC credentials and do not always require a pre-existing job offer.

## How the process works

1. Check which stream fits your situation using the BC PNP eligibility tool on WelcomeBC.
2. Create a profile and register in the Skills Immigration Registration System.
3. Wait for a draw. BC periodically invites the highest-scoring registered candidates to apply.
4. If invited, submit a full application with supporting documents within the deadline.
5. If approved, BC issues a nomination certificate.
6. Apply to the federal government for permanent residence using your certificate.

## Things people get wrong

- Assuming the score thresholds stay the same. Scores move with each draw, and what worked last month may not be enough today.
- Submitting a job offer letter that does not meet BC's employer requirements. The offer must be from an eligible employer at a qualifying wage for the right occupation.
- Missing the application window after receiving an invitation. Invitations have a hard deadline, typically 30 days.
- Overlooking the settlement funds requirement, which is separate from your employment income and varies by stream.
