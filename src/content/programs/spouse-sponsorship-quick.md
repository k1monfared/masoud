## In plain English

If you are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident and you are married or in a long-term committed partnership, you can sponsor your partner to join you in Canada as a permanent resident. It is one of the more reliable immigration pathways because Canada values keeping families together. You do not need a job offer, a language test, or a points score. You just need to show you are in a real relationship and that you meet a few basic sponsor requirements.

## Who this is for

Canadian citizens and permanent residents who want their spouse, common-law partner, or conjugal partner to live with them in Canada permanently. Whether your partner is already in Canada or living abroad, there is a path. The process and timing differ slightly depending on where your partner is when you apply.

## What you need

A genuine relationship that qualifies as a legal marriage, a common-law partnership (lived together for at least 12 consecutive months), or a conjugal partnership (unable to live together due to factors outside your control). The sponsor must be 18 or older, must not be receiving social assistance, and must not be disqualified by a prior sponsorship default. Both partners need to provide clear evidence that the relationship is real.

## How the process works

1. Determine whether you are applying inland (partner is in Canada) or outland (partner is abroad).
2. Gather relationship evidence and sponsor eligibility documents.
3. Submit the sponsorship and permanent residence applications. Inland applicants can include an open work permit request.
4. IRCC reviews the sponsor file, then processes the permanent residence application including biometrics and any requested interviews.
5. If approved, your partner lands as a permanent resident.

## Things people get wrong

- Thin relationship evidence. A short letter and a few photos are not enough. Build a clear record showing the relationship at different points in time.
- Misunderstanding common-law. Dating for a year is not the same as living together for 12 consecutive months, which is what the rule requires.
- Overlooking disqualifications. If you sponsored a prior spouse who became a permanent resident less than 5 years ago, you cannot sponsor again yet.
- Applying without addressing complications. If there are prior marriages, criminal records, or immigration history that could raise questions, talk to a professional before submitting.
