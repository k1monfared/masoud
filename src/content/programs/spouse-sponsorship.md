## In plain English

If you are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, you can sponsor your spouse or partner to come to Canada as a permanent resident. Canada puts family unity at the center of its immigration system, so this is one of the more reliable pathways. That said, IRCC looks carefully at every couple to make sure the relationship is real. If you have been together, living together, and building a life together, that is exactly what they want to see.

## Who this is for

Canadian citizens or permanent residents who are in a genuine marriage, common-law partnership, or conjugal partnership. The sponsored person can be anywhere in the world (outland) or already in Canada (inland). Both routes lead to the same outcome: permanent residence for your partner.

## What you need

The sponsor needs to be at least 18 years old, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and not disqualified by previous sponsorship defaults or certain criminal convictions. The relationship must be one of the three recognized types: legal marriage, common-law (living together for at least 12 continuous months), or conjugal (unable to cohabit due to circumstances beyond your control). Both partners need to provide substantial documentation showing the relationship is genuine, including photos, communication records, and evidence of shared life.

## How the process works

1. Confirm you meet the sponsor requirements and that your relationship qualifies under one of the three recognized types.
2. Gather relationship evidence: photos from different stages, communication logs, shared documents, statutory declarations from people who know you as a couple.
3. Submit the sponsorship application. If applying inland, submit both the sponsorship and the permanent residence application at the same time. An open work permit application can be included.
4. IRCC reviews the sponsor eligibility first, then processes the permanent residence application.
5. The sponsored person may be called for an interview or asked to provide biometrics.
6. If approved, the sponsored person receives Confirmation of Permanent Residence and can land.

## Things people get wrong

- Submitting thin relationship evidence. A few photos and a short letter are not enough. IRCC wants a timeline of the relationship with documentation from different periods and contexts.
- Assuming common-law is automatic after 1 year of dating. The 12-month cohabitation rule means physically living together, not just being in a relationship.
- Forgetting that previous sponsorships affect eligibility. If you sponsored a prior spouse or partner and that person became a permanent resident less than 5 years ago, you cannot sponsor a new partner yet.
- Not flagging complications upfront. Criminal records, prior immigration violations, or unusual relationship circumstances should be addressed with legal help before applying, not discovered during processing.
