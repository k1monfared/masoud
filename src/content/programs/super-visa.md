## In plain English

The Super Visa is Canada's way of letting you spend real, extended time with your parents or grandparents without the constant back-and-forth of renewing short-stay visits. Instead of being limited to 6 months, your parents can stay for up to 5 years at a stretch. The big requirement is Canadian medical insurance: it is not optional and it has to be in place before the visa is issued. We help you navigate the income proof, the insurance shopping, and the invitation letter so the application goes in complete the first time.

## Who this is for

This program is for parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens or permanent residents. If you want your family to stay long enough to actually feel at home rather than just visiting, this is the right path. It is also a good option for families where childcare or eldercare is part of the picture.

## What you need

- A signed invitation letter from your child or grandchild in Canada
- Proof that the Canadian host meets the minimum household income threshold for their family size
- Valid Canadian medical insurance worth at least CAD 100,000, covering at least 1 full year
- A valid passport
- Completed application forms and a recent photograph
- Biometrics if required for your nationality
- Results of an immigration medical examination from an IRCC-designated panel physician

## How the process works

1. Confirm that the Canadian child or grandchild qualifies as a host and meets the income requirement.
2. Purchase a qualifying Canadian medical insurance policy. Keep proof of the policy.
3. Have the host prepare an invitation letter with their income proof and a copy of their Canadian status document.
4. Book and complete an immigration medical exam with a panel physician.
5. Gather all documents and submit the Super Visa application online or at a Visa Application Centre.
6. Provide biometrics if required.
7. Wait for a decision. The officer may ask for more documents.
8. On approval, travel to Canada. The border officer sets the length of each stay on entry.

## Things people get wrong

- Buying the wrong insurance. Some policies look right on the surface but exclude pre-existing conditions or do not meet the minimum coverage amount. Read the policy before you pay.
- Income proof that is out of date. Officers want to see recent tax documents, not a Notice of Assessment from 3 years ago.
- Forgetting the medical exam. Many applicants leave this to the last minute and miss their planned travel date.
- Not showing ties to the home country. Even though the Super Visa allows long stays, the applicant still needs to show they plan to return eventually.
