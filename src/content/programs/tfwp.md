## In plain English

The Temporary Foreign Worker Program exists for situations where a Canadian employer genuinely cannot find a Canadian worker to fill a role. The employer has to prove that by advertising the job and showing the results. If Service Canada agrees, it issues an LMIA, which the worker then uses to apply for a closed work permit. Closed means you can only work for that employer, in that job, at that location. It is more rigid than an open permit, but for many workers it is the only path to working legally in Canada.

## Who this is for

This program is for foreign nationals who have a job offer from a specific Canadian employer in a role that the employer cannot fill with Canadian workers. It covers a wide range of occupations from high-skilled professional roles to low-wage positions in food processing, agriculture, and hospitality. Employers in industries with structural labour shortages, like meat processing, seasonal agriculture, and long-term care, use this program regularly.

## What you need

As a worker, you need a job offer letter with details matching the approved LMIA, qualifications that meet the position requirements, and admissibility documents including a passport, any required medical exam, and a background check if applicable. The employer handles the LMIA process on their end, including advertising, fees, and the Service Canada application. You apply for the work permit after the LMIA is issued.

## How the process works

1. The employer advertises the position to Canadians and documents the recruitment effort.
2. The employer applies to Service Canada for an LMIA, paying the applicable fee and providing the job details.
3. Service Canada reviews the application and issues a positive or negative LMIA.
4. With a positive LMIA, you apply to IRCC for a closed work permit.
5. If approved, IRCC issues a work permit naming your employer, location, and occupation.
6. You enter Canada and begin work under the conditions stated on your permit.

## Things people get wrong

- Believing the LMIA is your work permit. It is not. The LMIA is just one piece of the puzzle. You still need to apply separately for a work permit.
- Taking a second job or working for a different employer without authorization. Your permit is employer-specific, and working outside it is a violation that can affect future applications.
- Assuming the employer's LMIA covers an indefinite period. LMIAs have a specific validity window, and you must apply for the work permit within that window.
- Not verifying the employer's LMIA before paying fees or making arrangements. Fraudulent job offers referencing fake LMIAs do exist. Verify the offer is genuine.
