## In plain English

A study permit is the government document that lets you legally attend school in Canada if your program is longer than six months. Think of it as the official permission slip. Getting accepted to a Canadian school is just the first step. The study permit is what you actually need in hand before you arrive. Most applications are submitted online and take anywhere from a few weeks to a few months, depending on where you are applying from.

## Who this is for

Anyone who wants to study at a Canadian college, university, or other designated institution for a program longer than six months needs a study permit. This includes undergraduate students, graduate students, college diploma students, and language school students whose programs meet the threshold. Minors studying at a primary or secondary school may have different requirements depending on where their parent or guardian lives.

## What you need

The core documents are an acceptance letter from a designated learning institution, proof that you can pay for your studies and living costs, and identity documents. In most cases you also need a Provincial Attestation Letter, which your school can help you get. Some applicants need a medical exam or police certificate depending on their country. If this is your first time applying for a Canadian visa or permit, you will likely need to give biometrics at a visa application centre in your country.

## How the process works

1. Receive your Letter of Acceptance from your DLI.
2. Get your Provincial Attestation Letter from your school or province if required for your program level.
3. Gather your financial documents, identity documents, and any required police or medical certificates.
4. Submit your study permit application online through your IRCC account.
5. Give biometrics at a local visa application centre if instructed.
6. Wait for a decision. If approved, you will receive a Letter of Introduction to present at the Canadian border, where the physical permit is issued.

## Things people get wrong

- Applying without the Provincial Attestation Letter when it is required. Your application will be returned or refused.
- Understating your finances. Officers look for real, accessible funds, not just a bank statement printed hours before submission.
- Failing to explain ties to your home country. Officers assess whether you plan to leave after graduating, and applicants who cannot show reasons to return home face more scrutiny.
- Waiting until the last minute. Apply as early as possible because processing times vary and your school's start date will not move.
