## In plain English

If you are in Canada and you are afraid of what would happen to you if you went back to your home country, you have the right to ask Canada for protection. This is called a refugee claim. Canada's system is formal: your case goes to an independent tribunal, the Refugee Protection Division, where a board member hears your story and decides whether you qualify for protection. It is not automatic and it is not simple, but the right to claim is real, and having proper help preparing your case makes a significant difference. We can explain the process, help you gather evidence, and prepare you for your hearing.

## Who this is for

This process is for people who are physically present in Canada and cannot safely return to their home country because of a genuine risk of persecution, torture, or serious harm. It covers people who arrived at the border, people who entered Canada on another status and realized they cannot safely return, and people whose situations changed after they arrived. It is not for people who simply want to stay in Canada longer or who are unhappy with a visa refusal.

## What you need

- A completed Basis of Claim form explaining why you fear return to your country
- Identity documents if available (passport, national ID, birth certificate)
- Any evidence supporting your fear: news articles, police reports, medical records, witness letters, or country condition documents
- Interpretation if you do not speak English or French (provided at hearings)

## How the process works

1. Make your claim at a CBSA port of entry on arrival, or at an IRCC inland office if you are already in Canada.
2. CBSA or IRCC reviews your claim for eligibility. Most claims are referred to the Refugee Protection Division.
3. You receive a package with forms to complete, including the Basis of Claim form. This form is your written account of why you need protection. It is one of the most important documents in your case.
4. A hearing is scheduled at the RPD. You present your evidence and answer questions from a board member.
5. The RPD issues a written decision. If accepted, you are a protected person and can apply for permanent residence.
6. If refused, review your options with a lawyer immediately. Timelines for appeal are short.

## Things people get wrong

- Waiting too long to get legal help. The Basis of Claim form and the hearing are best handled with proper preparation, not improvised.
- Incomplete or vague Basis of Claim forms. A form that does not explain the specific events and specific fear gives the board member little to work with.
- Missing hearing deadlines. The RPD sets strict timelines. Missing them can result in abandonment of your claim.
- Not bringing evidence to the hearing. Personal testimony matters, but corroborating documents, country condition reports, and expert letters strengthen a case considerably.
