## In plain English

Most study permit refusals come down to the same handful of problems: thin proof of funds, a letter of explanation that does not connect your past to your future plans, or missing evidence that you intend to leave Canada when your studies end. None of these are hard problems to fix, but they need to be addressed before you submit, not after you get a refusal.

A study permit refusal can delay an academic plan by months. Most refusals come from a small set of recurring issues.

## 1. Weak proof of funds

Bank statements that show recent large deposits without a paper trail trigger questions. The officer needs to see funds that are clearly yours and have been with you long enough to indicate genuine availability. Plan three to six months ahead.

## 2. Letter of explanation that does not address purpose of study

The officer is assessing whether the program fits your career trajectory. A letter that lists facts but does not connect the dots from past work, to chosen program, to future plans, does not answer the question the officer is asking.

## 3. Missing or misaligned ties to home country

Ties to your home country signal intent to leave Canada at the end of your authorized stay. Property, family, employment commitments, ongoing obligations. If they exist, document them. If they do not, talk through alternative ways to address dual intent.

## 4. Choosing a program that is a clear step down

Applying for a program that is academically below your existing credentials, without a strong reason, raises a flag. There can be valid reasons. They need to be in the application.

## 5. Submitting before the file is ready

A complete application is faster than a fast application. Missing documents or unsigned forms do not get held with a polite note. They get refused.
